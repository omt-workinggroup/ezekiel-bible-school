How we teach

Classes are small and most of them meet around a table rather than in a lecture hall. You will be expected to read a lot, write regularly and be ready to explain a passage to people who did not come to hear a lecture.

Scripture

Every student reads the whole Bible in their first year and takes at least one term of Greek.

Formation

Groups of eight meet weekly with a tutor to pray, talk honestly and hold each other to account.

Practice

From the second term you serve with a partner church, preaching, teaching or visiting.

Admissions

We look for people who are already serving in their church and want to be better equipped for it. You don't need a theology background. You do need a recommendation from your pastor.

  1. Send an enquiry

    We'll post or email a prospectus and put you in touch with an admissions tutor.

  2. Apply

    A short form, a one-page testimony and your pastor's letter.

  3. Interview

    Thirty minutes, in person or on a video call.

  4. Accept your offer

    Bursary decisions are sent with offers, so you know the full cost before you commit.

Questions we get asked

Can I study online from another country?

Yes. Lectures are recorded and seminars run at two times each week so that most time zones are covered.

How do bursaries work?

Tick the bursary box on your application. We look at household income and your church's support, and most awards cover between 25% and 75% of fees.

Is the school tied to a denomination?

No. Students come from Anglican, Baptist, Pentecostal, Methodist and independent churches. Everyone affirms our statement of faith.

Is there accommodation?

Shared rooms on campus with meals included. We have six flats for married students, allocated in order of acceptance.

Can I transfer credit from another college?

Usually, for up to a third of a programme. Send your transcript with your application and the Dean will review it.
